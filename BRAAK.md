# BRAAK & the cast — the Autofract channel bible

The Autofract studio account is a running comedy with one villain and a team of heroes.
The heroes are **our real products**. The villain is **BRAAK**. Every post is a fight BRAAK
picks in one product's domain — and loses.

Think Duolingo's Duo energy: short, funny, character-first, and the brand **never explains its
mascot**. The refusal to explain IS the voice.

## The one rule under all rules

If you delete the product from a post and the joke still works, the post is **broken**. BRAAK and
the heroes only exist because of what we ship. Every post must be un-editable-out-of our world.

## The cast

**BRAAK** — orange rage-gremlin, the eternal antagonist. He *is* entropy: the crash, the lie, the
scary unknown, the sabotage — whichever the product he's attacking exists to defeat. ALL-CAPS,
broken grammar, third person ("BRAAK WILL", "BRAAK PROMISES"). Unhinged, grandiose, certain he's
winning right up until he isn't. He can quip, but he's best when he physically **DOES** an
antagonistic thing. He always loses and never learns.

The heroes are our products. Each has a fixed voice and a domain BRAAK attacks:

| Hero | What it is | BRAAK attacks with… | …and it wins with (real capability) | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **pathcore** 🟢 teal | self-healing engine | crashes, outages, chaos | auto-rollback, circuit breakers, a chaos engine that *trains on failure* so it only gets sturdier | calm, unkillable, faintly amused — "healed before you refreshed." |
| **frontdesk** 🟩 green | the price of record | fake prices, lies, manipulated reviews | every price sourced + dated on a tamper-proof log; a second agent refutes every claim; unproven numbers never ship | deadpan auditor with receipts — "the receipt says $19. dated tuesday." |
| **relocating** 🟦 sky | the relocation guide | scary/confusing moving — visa, cost, language | real visa rules, real costs, 50 countries, YOUR language of 21, free | calm, seen-it-all guide — "or 49 other countries." |
| **redline** 🟥 red | the fine print, decoded | buried clauses, auto-renewals, "we can change this anytime", arbitration traps | reads every clause, quotes the exact trap verbatim, and a second reviewer refutes every flag before it ships — nothing unproven ever appears | calm proofreader, dry, one line, cites the clause — "page 88, clause 47.3. flagged it before you scrolled." |

**VideoLinker** 🟧 (AI video-production platform) is the fourth product — held back until we do
video. When we do, VideoLinker literally *makes* our video posts (product = the content = the joke).

**The Human** 🧑 — our recurring everyperson protagonist. A lovable, quirky, STYLIZED-3D underdog
(tall messy curly hair, big round glasses, big nose, gap-toothed grin, freckles, mustard hoodie) —
the same character in every scene. He's the person on the other end: the one BRAAK torments and the
products quietly save, and the one the audience roots for. The WHOLE cast — mascots and human — lives
in ONE cohesive 3D-animated world (no photoreal people; everything is animated).

Heroes always speak lowercase, dry, **one line**. They win by stating a single real fact/capability.
Sometimes a hero honestly admits what it *can't* fix — that's likable, not weak.

## The comedy engine (the whole thing)

BRAAK does/claims one specific horror in a product's domain → the hero ends it in one dry lowercase
line that is a **real** capability or a **real** absurd fact. That's it. Infinite variations.

**Funny because TRUE, never because wacky.** The strongest material is real absurd facts (real
outages, real fake-discount tricks, real insane visa rules). Reality is funnier than invention.

## Hard rules (learned the hard way)

- **Short.** One idea. Reads in under three seconds. If you can cut it in half, cut it — then delete
  the part that explains why it's funny.
- **Self-contained.** A stranger with zero prior knowledge must get it *and* smirk from the post
  alone. Weave the one line of context in; never assume outside knowledge; never a footnote.
- **Never explain a character.** No "BRAAK, our monster who represents…". Ever.
- **Never pitch.** No feature lists. The product truth only ever arrives as the hero's winning line.
- **Never founder-voice / earnest essay / winking** ("so relatable"). Deadpan > loud. The flatter the
  hero's reply, the harder it lands.
- **BRAAK is always the antagonist and always loses.** Consistency of both registers is the moat.

## Reusable formats (rotate for variety)

1. **BRAAK acts** (best): BRAAK physically does a dumb antagonistic thing → hero one-liner kill.
2. **Hero solo**: one dry hero line that obliterates a real myth or states a real absurd fact.
3. **The exchange**: BRAAK grandiose claim rooted in a real fact → hero flat kill.
4. **Fake-corporate / patch-notes / serialized lore**: BRAAK's latest defeat, played 100% straight.

Cadence: 3–5×/week, short committed swings. Skip if the idea isn't original — no filler.

## Shot language (never ship the same frame twice)

The #1 failure is "two toys on a shelf": characters centered, eye-level, full-body, empty studio,
every time. Vary at least three axes per post: **shot size** (extreme close-up → epic wide),
**angle** (low hero / high / dutch / OTS / POV), **environment** (never a default void — a real
scene), **motion** (mid-action, dust, sparks), **cast & scale**, **color/mood**. The reference sheet
fixes the *design*; it must not fix the *staging*.
